The Index Is Hiding the Market

Hi,

The Federal Reserve raised interest rates this week for the first time since 2023. The quarter-point increase mattered, but the market's reaction revealed something more interesting.

The Dow fell 1.7%, the S&P 500 finished almost flat and the Nasdaq gained 0.7%. Large technology companies held the headline indices close to their highs while smaller companies and several cyclical sectors kept losing ground underneath them.

Week of September 14-18, 2026

S&P 500: around 7,650, down 0.1%

Nasdaq: around 26,520, up 0.7%

Dow: 51,682.64, down 1.7%

Bitcoin: around $81,000 after a volatile 6% weekly gain

What mattered

An index is not a democratic vote on the economy. Its largest companies can overwhelm what is happening across the rest of the market. This week, strength near the top concealed persistent weakness in smaller and more rate-sensitive businesses.

Narrow leadership is not proof that a correction is imminent. It does mean the market has fewer places to absorb disappointment if one of its largest companies stumbles.

A market can sit near a record while the average stock quietly loses ground. The index and the market are not always telling the same story.

My take

The Fed is tightening into an inflation problem driven partly by energy and geopolitics. Higher rates can weaken demand, but they cannot produce oil, reopen shipping routes or repair infrastructure. They fight the second-round effects by making the rest of the economy more expensive to finance.

For businesses, that creates a demanding combination: higher transport and input costs alongside more expensive capital. Strong balance sheets and pricing power become more valuable when revenue growth alone no longer guarantees that expansion creates value.

One lesson

A headline number is a starting point, not a conclusion. Whether you are reading an index, a company's revenue or your own monthly results, inspect what is carrying the total. An aggregate can be accurate and still conceal the change that matters most.
