Less Bad Was Good Enough

Hi,

Markets spent most of the shortened week bracing for the wrong kind of inflation surprise.

Oil was rising, yields were climbing and producer prices had already come in hot. Then CPI arrived close to expectations. It was not a clean victory over inflation, but it was not the upside shock investors had spent four days preparing for.

Week of September 8-11, 2026

S&P 500: 7,656.98, down 0.8% for the week

Nasdaq: 26,333.04, down 0.7%

Dow: 52,573.29, down 1.6%, its worst week since March

Bitcoin: around $78,700, still held below $80,000 resistance

What mattered

The revealing part was not simply the CPI number. It was the reaction. Friday's rally happened because inflation was less bad than feared, not because the inflation problem disappeared.

That distinction matters. Producer prices, oil and long yields still left the Federal Reserve with a difficult decision. Equities liked the absence of a shock. Bonds still saw enough pressure to keep another rate hike firmly on the table.

A relief rally is not the same thing as a healthy rally. It tells you what investors feared, not necessarily what has improved.

My take

The market avoided the worst version of the inflation story. It did not escape the story.

Oil is the part rates cannot fix cleanly. Higher interest rates can cool demand, but they cannot create barrels of oil, reopen shipping routes or resolve geopolitical conflict. If energy pressure stays high long enough, the issue moves from macro commentary into company margins, pricing decisions and household spending.

One lesson

Relief is not the same as good news. A bad month that is less bad than expected can feel like progress, but the useful question is whether the situation actually improved or expectations simply got worse beforehand.
