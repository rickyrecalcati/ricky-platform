The Calm Market and the Weaker Consumer

Hi,

The market ended the week looking almost too comfortable.

The S&P 500 posted its third straight weekly gain, small caps rallied, and volatility drifted lower. On the surface, investors looked relaxed. Underneath, the consumer data was harder to dismiss.

Week of August 10-14, 2026

S&P 500: 7,785.76, up 0.4% for the week

Nasdaq: 26,729.16, up 0.1%

Dow: 53,732.41, down 0.6%

Gold: around $4,375/oz, near a 10-week high

What mattered

July CPI rose 0.1% month over month and 3.4% year over year, matching expectations. Producer prices also eased more than expected. That gave investors a cleaner path to believe the Federal Reserve has less reason to keep tightening.

Then retail sales fell 0.6% in July, against expectations for a small gain. Consumer sentiment weakened as well. Cooling inflation is helpful. A cooling consumer is a more complicated signal.

Records at the index level can sit on top of a much shakier picture underneath.

My take

Markets have been assuming they can get the best version of the rate-cut story: inflation cools, the Fed steps back, and growth remains strong enough to protect earnings.

Friday's consumer data did not break that story, but it made it less comfortable. Rate cuts caused by easing inflation are one thing. Rate cuts caused by a weakening consumer are another.

One lesson

A rally built on falling volatility is not the same as a rally built on strength.

Calm can mean confidence. It can also mean investors have stopped looking closely. This week, the index said one thing and the consumer data whispered another.
